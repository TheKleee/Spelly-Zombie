# SPELLY ZOMBIE — THE GAME (ruled 2026-07-20/21)

The design Marko settled in one long night. Supersedes the earlier
"escape the village with the cauldron" premise. Spell system lives in
SPELL_PARTICLES.md; this is the game around it.

---

## One sentence

**The pot is your ink. Feed it, drag it across the village, and break the
thing the dead come from.**

## The filter every idea passes or fails

Familiar frame + exactly ONE novel thing (PEAK is climbing, Meccha
Chameleon is hide-and-seek). Frame = "zombies are coming, break the
source." Novelty = **drawing spells**. Nothing else may compete for that
slot.

Corollary Marko ruled: there are two kinds of complexity — the kind you
must be TOLD (villagers with roles and quotas: cut) and the kind you
DISCOVER (the rune grammar: kept). Depth is fine; required reading is not.

---

## The cauldron — one object, three jobs

1. **Ammo.** The only place a wand refills. No pot, no magic.
2. **Bait.** The dead only rouse when it is near — you must bring it into
   danger to make anything happen.
3. **The protected thing.** Everything that goes wrong, goes wrong here.

It cannot be destroyed. It can be **tipped** (spills fill) and **stolen**
(dragged off). Both are recoverable — spilled ink can be scooped back.

Ore in a backpack is *potential*; only the cauldron converts it to ink.

## Moving the cauldron — ink levitation

**THE CHARIOT IS CUT.** There is no vehicle and there never will be. It was
removed long ago; nothing named Chariot or Backpack exists in the project.

**You haul the pot by drawing ink on it and levitating it.** That is the
mechanic the chariot used to be, so it carries the game the way the mountain
carries PEAK: if hauling does not feel great, nothing underneath catches the
game.

- **Nothing has HP here.** Players and the cauldron both have none.
- **Upgradeable by attaching solid objects to it with Sticky** — you conjure or
  find a solid thing and stick it on. Same verb, new surface, and the run's
  history is written on the pot itself.

## Zombies — thieves and repairmen, not predators

**They do not attack players at all.** They run away from you. They cannot cast
and they have no special attack. They exist to undo your work:

- **CURSE THE CAULDRON to remove its ink.** This is their entire offense.
- **They swarm you ONLY when you have lost your wand completely.**
- squabble with each other for no reason (they are stupid)

**CUT, never built, do not reintroduce:** the soap zombie that erases your ink,
the sack zombie that steals the pot, and the zombie that carries fragments back
to repair the Ink Ore. None of those are in the game.

**The causal chain is the whole game's pressure:** curse the pot → no clean ink →
you cannot refill → your wand dissolves → wandless → NOW they swarm you. Zombies
never threaten you directly. They threaten your supply, and the supply is what
keeps them afraid of you.

**No downed system. No player HP loss from zombies.** Danger comes from
the world: a crowd is a *stampede* — it jostles, tramples, and shoves you
off ledges, into water, into your own fire. Zombies never kill you; they
arrange for the environment to do it.

Rewards on death: **ore** (ink) and **rune pages** (new spells) — so
fighting is an opportunity, never merely an obstacle.

## The journey — environment is the obstacle, spells are the solution

Drawing is slow, so obstacles must be **patient**: blocked streets,
flooded ground, hills, ledges, sealed doors. They wait for you; combat
does not. This is the resolution of "casting is more interesting than
fighting."

**LAW: never a one-rune lock.** Every obstacle must have several valid
answers (freeze the water, bridge it with Solid, or shove the cart across
with force). One answer = a key hunt = a chore. Many answers = players
feel clever, and every rune stays useful.

Example the whole design earns: **Slick cast under the chariot makes it
slide.**

## Zones — camps, graveyards, root nodes

Optional, player-initiated. You attack them; they do not come to you.
This is what gives a slow verb room: **see it from a distance, prepare,
then commit.** Plan → execute → chaos is the comedy engine.

- **A carved stone at the entrance shows which rune the zone drops** —
  the choice becomes informed and social ("we need Solid for that bridge").
  Entering blind is allowed too.
- **One rune drops per zone.** Whoever picks it up learns it. Crews
  negotiate; solo play is unaffected.
- **How deep you park the pot is the difficulty dial** — edge of the camp
  wakes a few, the middle wakes everything. Set physically, no menu.
- **Escalation: the more zones you raid, the more the dead retaliate** —
  more ambushes, more attempts on the pot. Your greed is the difficulty
  setting. It must be *visible* (thicker haze, louder groaning) or the
  spike reads as arbitrary.

**Ambush rules** (or the slack that makes the game funny dies):
telegraph a beat before (the "oh no" IS the fun) · threaten time and
resources, never lives · always escapable · timed for when you are
committed (mid-pull, narrow street, right after spending your ink).

## The Large Ink Ore — the destination

Visible from the first minute, like PEAK's summit. **The gate is soft:**
you *can* attack it immediately, you will just get wrecked — bold crews
failing teaches better than a locked door.

Its **roots run under the village**; the dead rise where the roots run,
and the smaller camps sit on root nodes. (Killing a node quiets its
district.)

**The fight:**

1. Park the pot at the ore — everything wakes.
2. Break the **shell** with spells. **It has no health bar — it physically
   breaks apart in chunks.**
3. **The fragments are your ink.** Scoop them into the pot to keep
   casting. The fight generates its own ammunition and escalates instead
   of running dry.
4. **Zombies carry fragments back to rebuild the shell.** Tug-of-war, not
   a grind — but **they may only rebuild the shell, never the core.**
   Phases ratchet: ground can be lost inside a phase, never the phase.
5. The shell gives → the **Ink Ore Core** is exposed. Everything wants it.
6. **The core is hand-carried and you cannot cast while holding it.** The
   carrier is defenseless and needs escorts. Get it to the cauldron.

**Score: how fast you broke it.**

## Backpack, not weapons

Carried weapons are CUT — they compete with the novelty (a sword is
faster and more familiar than drawing, so players would swing instead of
cast) and the design orphaned them anyway (zombies do not attack players).

- The old weapon slots become **backpack slots** — ore, fragments, planks.
- **Pack = logistics. Hands = drama.** The core and the cauldron are
  hand-carried and block casting.
- **Conjured items stay** (obsidian blade etc.): earned by a spell, not
  equipped from a menu.
- Out of ink? Grab and throw physics objects. Slapstick, free.
- A downed/knocked player **spills their pack**.

## Teaching — no text anywhere

The **wand is the tutorial**. Marko's ink-gauge sketch does the work:

draw → the wand visibly shrinks → it runs out → you cannot cast → the only
ink is the cauldron → the cauldron is empty → the glowing rocks are right
there.

Each step is a felt lack pointing at the next. **Start nearly empty, in
the light, before anything is chasing you** — the lesson lands in the
first thirty seconds while it is safe. One player figuring it out teaches
the lobby.

**Sequencing rule: the first zombie arrives AFTER the first successful
cast.** Feel powerful once, then be threatened.

**Grimoire pages** carry pictographic equations (no words, every language
free). The page that matters most is not ore+cauldron — it is
**rune + circle = cast**, the one step nothing else teaches. Rune art can
be Marko's own recorded strokes (the display layer already prefers them).

## Cut tonight, and why

- **Villagers / rescue quotas** — a second thing to learn; competes with
  the drawing for the novelty slot.
- **Global nightfall** — that is Minecraft's language. Danger is now
  *local*: green-black fog around camps and root nodes, so safe and unsafe
  exist at the same time and you can see the difference from a street away.
- **Carried weapons** — see above.
- **Player death by zombie / downed system** — the environment kills.

## Open questions

1. Does destroying the core end the run on the spot (source consumed, the
   dead drop), or trigger a final escape? Ending on the biggest moment is
   the stronger shape.
2. Fiction: this is now a **raid**, not an escape. Confirmed shift.
3. Multiplayer remains the biggest unproven risk; the map does not exist
   yet; spells must actually feel good (liquids/solids are load-bearing
   now, not polish).
