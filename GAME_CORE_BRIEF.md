# SPELLY ZOMBIE: what the game is (brief for the marketing agent)

Verified against the design record on 2 September 2026. Release is this year, very soon. This is the current game and the only mode. Everything older (co-op raid, chariot, souls, conversion, team switching, escort mode, weapons, ink ore) was cut and must never be mentioned.

## One line

A hide-and-seek party game where you cast spells by drawing them, played as Wizards vs Acolytes: prop hunt meets capture the flag, and the flag is a cauldron of ink.

## The one novel thing: you draw your magic

- Magic is ink. Every player holds a wand full of ink and draws with it on anything: walls, floors, objects, the air in front of you, even your own body.
- Draw a rune, close it inside a seal, and the spell exists. There are 12 runes in 6 opposite pairs: Heat and Chill, Light and Dark, Solid and Liquid, Sticky and Slick, Compress and Spread, Attract and Repel. The glyphs are hand drawn shapes you learn to draw, not buttons.
- Runes combine. Heat plus Light is Flame. Chill plus Light is Heal. Slick plus Compress is Teleport. Flame plus Solid is a Meteor. Bigger drawings make bigger spells. One rune alone hits a target, a stronger drawing makes an area, the strongest one turns the ground itself into a temporary biome, a patch of frost inside a fire.
- Both sides draw, so nothing competes with the novelty. Wizards draw attack magic. Acolytes draw the dead.
- Wizards can also paint spells onto their own bodies and pose to trigger them.
- The wand is the only gauge: it visibly shrinks as ink is spent and melts to nothing when empty. No bars, no numbers, no interface to read.

## The two sides

### Wizards (the seekers)
- Strong, fast learners, the fun version of the hunter that prop hunt games usually make boring.
- Start each round knowing only Attract and Repel. Every other rune is learned by absorbing objects in the world with the grimoire: flames teach Heat, puddles teach Liquid and Chill, rocks teach Solid and Dark. Absorbing destroys the object, so a wizard leaves a trail of missing torches. The map is a finite school.
- Their spells are powerful enough that a careless wizard hurts himself. That self-damage is the acolyte's opening.
- Their ink refills from the cauldron while it is theirs, fastest when close, a trickle from far away. The whole team orbits the pot: drift in empty, drift out full.

### Acolytes (the hiders)
- Corrupted wizards with a green wand and a strange grimoire, 70 percent the size of a wizard, full speed, and a jump twice their height.
- Cannot fight a wizard head on, by design. They scheme.
- Aim at any liftable object and scan it, and you become it on the spot. A crate, a barrel, a bucket, a chair. Turn back with one key. Disguise is a tool for a moment of hiding when wizards get close, not a way to win.
- Leaving a disguise puffs a small poison cloud, so wizards learn to blast suspicious objects from a distance instead of walking up to them. Prop paranoia is the point.
- They raise zombies by drawing. Solid raises a melee zombie with a poison cloud, Liquid raises a ranged zombie that lobs corruption at the cauldron, the Attract arrow tells them where to go, the Repel glyph spreads them out. Up to 10 zombies each.
- Acolytes start with no runes at all. They earn each one by doing things: scan something, turn back into yourself, summon a zombie, keep two alive at once. Eight more mischief spells unlock from deeds like getting a wizard to stand next to you while you are a chair for three seconds: decoys that run away, objects that explode into poison when a wizard blasts them, a curse that swaps a wizard's grimoire for an acolyte one for 30 seconds, ink that evaporates other ink.
- Zombies are afraid of wizards who hold a wand and flee from anyone chasing them, but they bite anyone who turns their back. Their job is to be chased, to drag wizards away from the pot. Killing one makes its owner whistle, giving away where the acolyte hides.
- An acolyte can draw a seal on their own zombie and detonate it in a wizard's face, or plant a slow triangle summon as a trap that pops ten seconds later on whoever walks in. Every trap leaves a visible mark, so there is always a counter play.

## The objective: the cauldron

- One pool of ink lives in a cauldron. Wizards own it black, acolytes want it green.
- Acolytes corrupt it by standing close for a few seconds, like planting a bomb. Wizards clean it by standing near it for about ten seconds, like defusing one. A tended green pot keeps drinking from the acolytes; an abandoned green pot slowly evaporates, and the pot can never be refilled. Every drop lost is lost for the round.
- While the pot is green the wizards' wands stop refilling and the acolytes drink from it instead. Losing the pot threatens every wand on the wizard team at once.
- There are three cauldrons on the map. Break the one holding the ink and the ink shoots into the sky as a comet and lands in another pot ten seconds later. Break all three and the ink falls flat at the map center where it can never move again, and the round becomes a siege.
- The pot is the scoreboard. A bar at the top of the screen shows its colour and how much ink is left, with a trophy over the face of whoever would win if the clock ran out now. Zero text.
- Either side can lift the cauldron with ink and carry it. Nobody can hide while hauling a cauldron.

## A round

1. Everyone spawns at the same time, wizards together at the center, acolytes alone and scattered out of sight.
2. About 30 seconds of prep: the pot is closed, wizards run out to absorb runes, acolytes scan shapes. Then the pot opens and the ink arrives from the sky.
3. The match runs 5 to 15 minutes, set by the host.
4. Endings:
   - the pot runs empty at any moment: acolytes win instantly
   - all wizards dead: acolytes win
   - time runs out with a green pot: acolytes win, even if every acolyte is already dead
   - time runs out with a clean pot and ink left: wizards win
   - all acolytes dead and the pot clean: wizards win on the spot
5. Death is a mode, not a menu. A dead player becomes a ghost that can possess their own team's spells: a wizard ghost rides a spell, an acolyte ghost drives a zombie and uses its abilities. A living teammate can revive you if both of you reach your body.

## The world: Spelly Island

- One island map, generated fresh from a daily seed, so it is familiar in shape and new in detail every match: a village center with houses you can enter and hide in, forest, beach, water, a frozen peak, stone fields.
- Every biome, object, creature and spell shares one set of ten properties: temperature, light, pressure, balance, state, affinity, strength, mind, courage, clones. A rock born by the volcano shrugs off heat; the same rock from the peak melts there. Objects teach the runes of the biome they stand in.
- The world reacts. Spells push those properties past their thresholds and things burn, freeze, blind, float, shatter, glue together, boil, or turn to stone. Cold slows you, darkness blinds you, a heavy body crouches. Fire spreads to what it touches.
- Everything is physical. Doors swing when you walk into them, lean gently and you peek through the crack. Props fly when hit. Golems rise from the debris of careless destruction and attack anyone, so nuking a field to find a hider breeds new enemies.
- Streets and trails are grown by the map generator to reach every house door.

## How it feels and sounds

- Friend slop: simple enough for kids, funny because of what happens, never because of wordplay. Deadpan. A wand melting is the joke.
- Almost no text on screen. The game teaches with pictures, glyphs, and emoji chips. Every player facing string is translated into 12 languages: English, Japanese, Simplified and Traditional Chinese, Korean, Spanish, French, Italian, German, Brazilian Portuguese, Russian, Turkish. Japanese first. Japanese title: スペリーゾンビ.
- No text chat, ever. Proximity voice only: if you want to say something, you reveal where you are. Hearing the enemy whisper is the comedy.
- Keep the game's nouns exactly: rune, seal, ink, wand, grimoire, zombie, wizard, acolyte, cauldron.

## Who it is for and why it works

- The Meccha Chameleon crowd, 12 million players who loved a simple hide-and-seek and burned out on it. Two things they complain about are the two things this game fixes: playing the hunter is boring here it is the powerful spell caster, and dead players sit in a menu here they become ghosts that keep playing.
- Witch Hat Atelier fans. Its magic system is drawn glyphs in circles with ink, and no game adaptation exists. The grimoire, the pointy hats, the cauldron all read at a glance.
- Friends first. The full game needs other people, so every player recruits. The built in bot opponent is deliberately terrible: it teaches the shape of a match and makes you want a real friend across the table.
- Clip factory by design: every combo and every failure should read in a 15 second clip. Slipping on your own oil, freezing your friend, a meteor drawn on a wall, a wizard blasting a barrel that turns out to be a person.
- Positioning line the developer uses: familiar but ours. Recognisable prop hunt structure, one new verb.

## Facts

- Developer: Marko, solo, eight years of games, based in Serbia. Spelly Zombie is his first market-first title.
- Engine and platform: Unity 6, Steam, PC (Windows, Mac, Linux builds).
- Players: online multiplayer, lobbies of 2 to 4 in the demo, larger later. Public lobby browser with region and language tags, private lobbies through Steam invites. No single player mode beyond the practice lobby and the bot match.
- The lobby is the tutorial: an immortal sandbox where you can switch sides freely and absorb every rune from immortal sources before a match.
- Release: 2026, very soon. The game launches on momentum, not on a festival calendar. Steam Next Fest is not part of the plan.
- Steam page: live immediately, demo public on the page ahead of launch.
- Steam Witch Fest: April 22 to 26, 2027, a themed sale the game qualifies for after launch (magic, potion making, pointy hats, grimoires).
- Goal stated by the developer: 100,000 downloads in the first month.
- Store tag template borrowed from Meccha Chameleon: Casual, Party Game, Multiplayer, Real Time Tactics, Hidden Object, Cute, Hand-drawn, Comedy, Mystery, Investigation, Stealth, Team-Based.

## Current build status (do not overpromise)

- In the build now: drawing and recognition of all 12 runes, seals, the spell ladder and fusions, the ten property world, absorbing runes from objects, acolyte transformation, zombies with commands and detonation, the cauldron economy with three pots and the ink comet, prep phase and referee with all five endings, ghosts and revives, the generated island with houses and interiors, the lobby sandbox, Steam lobbies with a browser.

## Rules for the agent

- Do not invent features, numbers, or lore. If something is not in this brief, ask.
- Marko writes every final sentence of store and marketing copy himself. Produce structure, angles, hooks, outlines, and drafts clearly marked as drafts.
- Language: simple, deadpan, situation based humour. No puns, no idioms, no exclamation marks, no dashes.
- Never describe the game as a first game or the developer as a beginner.
- Never call it Counter-Strike with a cauldron. The frame is prop hunt plus capture the flag.
