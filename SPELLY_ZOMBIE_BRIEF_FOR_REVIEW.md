# Spelly Zombie: a brief for an outside opinion

Written 14 September 2026 by the developer's assistant, checked against the current design. Please read all of it before answering, then answer the questions at the end. Everything here is the game as it stands today; nothing is aspirational unless marked.

## One line

A hide-and-seek party game where you cast spells by drawing them, played as Wizards vs Acolytes. Prop hunt meets capture the flag, and the flag is a cauldron of ink.

## The one new thing: you draw your magic

- Every player holds a wand of ink and draws with it on anything: walls, floors, objects, the air, your own body.
- There are 12 runes in 6 opposite pairs: Heat and Chill, Light and Dark, Solid and Liquid, Sticky and Slick, Compress and Spread, Attract and Repel. They are hand drawn shapes you learn, not buttons. Draw one, close it inside a seal, and the spell exists.
- Runes combine. Heat plus Light is Flame. Chill plus Light is Heal. Flame plus Solid is a Meteor. Bigger drawings make bigger spells: one rune hits a target, a stronger drawing makes an area, the strongest turns the ground itself into a temporary biome.
- Drawing is charging. A sloppy rune still works, a careful one is stronger. If you stop halfway, the book can finish the line for you.
- The wand is the only gauge. It visibly shrinks as ink is spent and dissolves when empty. No bars, no numbers.

## The two sides

**Wizards, the seekers.** Strong, fast learners, the fun version of the hunter. They start a match knowing only Attract and Repel and learn every other rune by absorbing objects in the world: a flame teaches Heat, a puddle teaches Liquid. Absorbing destroys the object, so a wizard leaves a trail. Their spells are strong enough to hurt themselves, and that carelessness is the acolyte's opening. Their ink refills only from the cauldron while it is theirs.

**Acolytes, the hiders.** Corrupted wizards with a green wand, smaller and higher jumping, who cannot win a straight fight. They scan any liftable object and become it on the spot, a crate, a barrel, a chair, and turn back with one key. Leaving a disguise puffs a poison cloud, so wizards learn to blast suspicious objects from a distance. Acolytes raise zombies by drawing: a melee one, a ranged one that lobs corruption at the cauldron, and two glyphs to send them or scatter them. Zombies flee wizards who hold a wand and bite anyone who turns their back; their job is to be chased. Acolytes start with no runes and earn every one by deeds: scan something, turn back, summon, keep two alive. Eight mischief spells unlock from further deeds, such as a decoy that runs away or a curse that swaps a wizard's grimoire for an acolyte one.

## The objective: the cauldron

One pool of ink in a cauldron. Wizards own it black, acolytes want it green. Acolytes corrupt it by standing close for a few seconds; wizards clean it by standing near it longer. While it is green, wizard wands stop refilling and the acolytes drink from it. Ink that leaves the pot is gone for the round. There are three cauldrons on the map: break the one holding the ink and the ink flies to another; break all three and the ink lies on the ground where the round becomes a siege. Either side can lift its own cauldron and carry it, and nobody can hide while carrying one. The pot is the scoreboard: a bar shows its colour and level, with a trophy over the face of whoever is winning right now.

## A match

Wizards spawn together in the centre, acolytes alone and scattered, each born into a biome. About 30 seconds of prep while the pot brews, then 5 minutes by default, the host can set up to 15. Five endings: the pot runs empty, acolytes win instantly; all wizards dead, acolytes win; time out with a green pot, acolytes win even from the grave; time out with a clean pot, wizards win; all acolytes dead and the pot clean, wizards win on the spot. Death is a mode, not a menu: a dead player becomes a ghost, and a living teammate standing at the body revives them.

## The world: Spelly Island

One island, generated fresh from a daily seed: a village centre with houses you can enter, forest, beach, a lake, stone fields and a frozen peak. Everything in it, biome, object, creature, spell, shares one set of ten properties: temperature, light, pressure, balance, state, affinity, strength, mind, courage, clones. Biomes are felt relative to the ground you were born on: the beach is hot and makes you bold, the forest is dark and makes you nervous, the peak freezes and slows you. Bravery and fear act on your eyes, not your legs: your gaze leans onto things or slides off them, so you always keep control. Spells push properties past thresholds and things burn, freeze, float, shatter, glue or turn to stone. Everything is physical, props fly when hit, and golems rise from the debris of careless destruction.

## Tone and rules of the house

Simple enough for kids, funny because of what happens, never because of wordplay. Deadpan. Almost no text on screen; the game teaches with pictures and glyphs, and every string ships in 12 languages. No text chat, proximity voice only, so speaking reveals where you are. The stated north star: prop hunt meets Witch Hat Atelier, funny like Gintama, with real stakes allowed because the baseline is silly.

## Who it is for and why

- The prop hunt crowd that burned out on simple hide-and-seek, who complain that the hunter is boring and that dead players sit in a menu. Here the hunter is the spell caster and the dead keep playing as ghosts.
- Witch Hat Atelier fans: its magic is drawn glyphs in ink, and no game adaptation exists.
- Friends first. Lobbies of 2 to 12, public with region and language tags, or private through Steam invites. The lobby is an immortal sandbox where you can switch sides and learn every rune before a match. The full game needs other people, so every player recruits. Split-screen was considered and rejected.

## Facts and status

- Solo developer, eight years of games, based in Serbia. Unity 6, Steam, PC first.
- Steam page going live now, launch planned for late October 2026 on momentum rather than a festival.
- In the build today: all 12 runes with recognition, seals and fusions, the ten property world, absorbing runes from objects, acolyte disguise and zombies, the cauldron economy with three pots, the referee with all five endings, ghosts and revives, the generated island with houses and interiors, the lobby sandbox, Steam and LAN lobbies, a first Windows build.
- Being finished now: a full multiplayer parity pass so everything seen in solo play shows on every machine, the eight acolyte mischief spell behaviours, sound, a trailer.
